--------------
1. Information
--------------
One rocket jump at your current view, chosen so that you land on an overbounce.

One rocket jump per key press, aimed where you are looking, chosen so that you land on an overbounce. Be on the ground with the rocket launcher ready and press the key. Jump and shot go through the usual randomized input layer.

You don't have to stand still: the jump can only go out a few frames after the key press, so the plan is made for where you will be by then (sliding to a stop or running on flat ground is carried forward pmove by pmove) and checked against where you really are in the frame the jump is sent. If that check fails it plans again, up to three times.

Added: 2026-11-10

-----------------
2. How it chooses
-----------------
At a fixed view the only free choice is how many pmove slots lie between the jump and the shot. Each one gives one knockback and therefore one landing, and only about one landing in twenty ends inside the 0.25 unit ground trace, so on its own an overbounce exists about a third of the time. The pitch may therefore also be nudged by up to mdd_rocketjump_overbounce_nudge degrees right before the shot; it is turned back afterwards.

Every candidate is simulated against the real map the way the server runs it: jump, muzzle point, 50 ms prestep, rocket impact, splash knockback (whole points only), velocity snapped every pmove, free fall until the landing. Of all candidates that overbounce, the highest one is used, typically 10-15 units below the best plain rocket jump. If none overbounces, the best plain rocket jump at your exact view is done and a line says so.

Aiming straight down (the engine stops the view at 87.89 degrees) is the hardest case: the view can only be tilted one way and the blast stays under your feet where the knockback barely changes. Use mdd_rocketjump_overbounce_nudge 4 there.

--------
3. Debug
--------
With mdd_rocketjump_overbounce_debug 1 every use prints the chosen candidate and checks it against the server: how many pmoves after the jump the shot went out, which usercmd the blast followed, the blast point and knockback, the velocity after the blast (MATCH / MISMATCH) and the landing, ending in one RESULT line. The same plus full detail is appended to rjob.log in the mod folder.

---------
4. Limits
---------
VQ3 physics, com_maxfps 125, pmove_fixed 1 / pmove_msec 8 (or g_synchronousClients 1). The jump is held back a frame or two until the shot can go out with a usercmd that ends exactly on the planned slot.

The blast timing is certain when the rocket lands within 45 units of the muzzle (roughly pitch 65 and steeper with a short delay); those candidates are preferred. Shallower shots are marked "timing depends on the frame phase" and can miss more often.

Don't move the mouse between the key press and the shot, and don't steer onto ground of a different height during the flight. Only normal overbounces are targeted, not sticky ones. Movers are not simulated. Online the server timing can shift by a usercmd, which misses.

-----------------
5. Usage examples
-----------------
bind mouse4 +mdd_rocketjump_overbounce
    Look at the floor in front of you with the rocket launcher, tap mouse4.

mdd_rocketjump_overbounce_nudge 4
    Aiming straight down: allow a bigger view correction.

mdd_rocketjump_overbounce_nudge 0
    Never touch the view: only the jump-to-shot delay is varied.

bind mouse4 "+mdd_rocketjump_overbounce"; bind space +mdd_overbounce_jump
    Land the overbounce, then hold space to jump out of it on the first possible usercmd.

mdd_rocketjump_overbounce_debug 1
    See the RESULT line and write rjob.log.
